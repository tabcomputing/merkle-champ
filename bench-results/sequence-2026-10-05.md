# Sequence prototype against Vector, 2026-10-05

Machine: Intel Core Ultra 7 155H, Linux, release build, pinned to one CPU
(`taskset -c 2`). One run of `examples/sequence_bench.rs`; each figure is the
median of seven samples after a warm-up, in nanoseconds per operation unless
marked. Random data. `Vector` has no insert or remove, so they are slice,
push and concat, which copy, as a user would have to.

`Sequence` is the content-defined (prolly) tree of `src/sequence.rs`: leaves
cut where a rolling hash allows, branches cut by the same hash, every edit a
join that re-chunks only until the new cuts meet the old ones.

## Reading

- **Structural edits:** insert, remove, concat and slice are 100 to 2,000
  times faster, and an insert rewrites 3 or 4 nodes instead of every leaf
  after it, so a store shares everything else between versions.
- **Reading:** iteration and the first identity are the same. A random `get`
  is about 6 times slower: a binary search over the children's lengths at
  each level, where the vector shifts bits.
- **Point update:** about 5 times slower, through a fast path that copies
  the leaf and its path when the new value moves no cut.
- **Building** is 2.5 to 3 times slower: every element is fingerprinted for
  the rolling hash.
- Tried and reverted: interpolation search in `get` (slower than binary
  search).
